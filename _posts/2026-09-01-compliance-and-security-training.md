Compliance and training serve different purposes, but for small and mid-size companies they solve the same underlying problem: your organization needs to demonstrate that it takes security seriously, and your people need to actually know how to do it. One without the other doesn't work.

## Compliance Readiness

### What It Is

Compliance readiness is the process of getting your organization prepared to pass a formal security audit — most commonly SOC 2 or PCI DSS. The audit itself is performed by a licensed CPA firm or QSA (Qualified Security Assessor). What we do is everything that happens *before* the auditor arrives.

### Why It Matters

If you sell to enterprise customers, you've probably been asked for a SOC 2 report. If you process credit card payments, you need PCI compliance. These aren't optional — they're deal-blockers. A prospect's security team will ask for your SOC 2 report, and if you don't have one, the deal stalls or dies.

The problem is that getting audit-ready is a multi-month project that touches your entire organization — policies, technical controls, access management, logging, vendor management, HR processes, incident response. Most companies without security staff don't know where to start, and the gap between "we should probably do SOC 2" and "we passed the audit" is where we come in.

### What the Engagement Looks Like

**Gap Assessment.** We evaluate your current security posture against the target framework and identify what's missing. This gives you a concrete list of what needs to change, not a vague sense of unreadiness.

**Policy Development.** You need written security policies. Not boilerplate downloaded from the internet — policies that actually reflect how your organization operates and that your team will follow.

**Control Implementation.** Turning policies into reality. Configuring logging and monitoring, implementing access controls, setting up endpoint protection, hardening cloud infrastructure. We do this alongside your team so they understand and can maintain what's been built.

**Evidence Collection.** Auditors want proof. We help you build the evidence collection process so that when the auditor asks "show me your access review from Q3," you can produce it in minutes, not days.

**Audit Liaison.** When the auditor arrives, we're in the room (or on the call) to answer technical questions, provide context, and keep the process moving.

### Timeline and Cost

SOC 2 readiness typically takes 3 to 6 months depending on your starting point. PCI varies based on your SAQ level. Engagements are usually fixed-fee, scoped after the gap assessment.

## Security Training

### What It Is

Hands-on training workshops for your engineering and operations teams. Not annual security awareness videos about phishing — practical, technical sessions built around real vulnerabilities and real-world attack scenarios.

### Why Slide Decks Don't Work

Most security training is designed for compliance evidence, not actual learning. Your developers sit through a generic presentation about the OWASP Top 10, check a box, and forget everything by the next day. That satisfies an auditor but doesn't make your code more secure.

Effective training is hands-on, specific to your tech stack, and taught by someone who has actually found and exploited the kinds of vulnerabilities being discussed. When a developer sees a live demonstration of SQL injection in code that looks like their own, it sticks in a way that a slide about "parameterized queries" never will.

### What We Cover

**Secure Coding Practices.** Specific to your language and framework. Not abstract principles — concrete patterns and anti-patterns drawn from real-world vulnerabilities.

**AI Security Awareness.** Your developers are already using AI tools — Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude. Do they understand the risks? Prompt injection, data leakage through AI context, insecure code suggestions, and how to evaluate AI output critically. If your product integrates AI, we also cover securing LLM pipelines, tool-calling exploits, and agent safety.

**Threat Modeling.** Teaching your development team to think about security during design, not after deployment. Practical exercises using your actual architecture.

**Cloud Security for Developers.** IAM, least privilege, secure defaults, and the most common misconfigurations in AWS, Azure, and GCP — framed for developers who provision infrastructure, not just security engineers.

### Format

Workshops are typically half-day or full-day sessions, available in person or remote. We build the content around your tech stack and your actual codebase where possible. Small group sizes work best — 8 to 20 people — so there's room for questions and discussion.

## Why They Go Together

The connection is simple: compliance requires evidence that your team is trained, and training is more effective when it's tied to real business requirements. When your developers understand *why* the company needs SOC 2 and *what the auditor will look for*, secure coding practices stop being abstract best practices and start being part of their job.

Companies that do both at the same time get the best results — the compliance process creates urgency and context for the training, and the training creates the cultural foundation that makes compliance sustainable year over year.
