If you run a small or mid-size company, you've probably heard that you need a CISO — a Chief Information Security Officer. Someone who owns your security strategy, talks to the board about risk, manages compliance, and leads the response when something goes wrong.

The problem is that a full-time CISO costs $200,000 to $350,000 a year in salary alone, before benefits, before the tools and team they'll want to build. For a company with 50 or 200 employees, that math doesn't work. But the security problems that require CISO-level thinking don't go away just because you can't afford the title.

That's what a Virtual CISO solves.

## How It Works

A Virtual CISO (vCISO) is an experienced security professional who serves as your organization's security leader on a part-time, retainer basis. You get the strategic thinking, the decision-making, and the accountability of a CISO — without the full-time headcount.

In practice, a vCISO engagement typically includes:

- **Security program ownership.** Someone who actually owns your security posture, reviews it regularly, and builds a roadmap for where it needs to go.
- **Architecture and design review.** Before your team ships a new product or feature, your vCISO reviews the design for security risks.
- **Compliance oversight.** Whether you're pursuing SOC 2, PCI, or just trying to pass customer security questionnaires, your vCISO manages the process.
- **Incident response.** When something goes wrong — a breach, a vulnerability disclosure, a compromised account — your vCISO is the first call and coordinates the response.
- **Vendor risk management.** Evaluating the security posture of your vendors and managing those relationships.
- **Board and leadership reporting.** Translating security risk into business language for non-technical leadership.

The time commitment varies. Most engagements are 10 to 20 hours per month, though some are more during active compliance pushes or after a security incident. It's a monthly retainer, typically running $3,000 to $10,000 per month depending on the company size and scope.

## Who Needs a vCISO?

The typical vCISO client is a company that has reached a point where security decisions are being made — by the CTO, the IT admin, or nobody at all — but no one in the room has security as their primary job. Common triggers:

- **A customer or prospect asks for your SOC 2 report** and you don't have one.
- **You're integrating sensitive data** (healthcare, financial, PII) and need someone to own the security architecture.
- **You've had a security incident** and realized nobody was in charge of the response.
- **Your cyber insurance application** asks questions nobody on your team can answer.
- **You're growing** and know you'll need a security program eventually, but aren't ready to hire a full-time leader.

If any of those sound familiar, you're the right size for a vCISO.

## What a vCISO Is Not

A vCISO is not a penetration tester, not an auditor, and not a managed security service. Those are project-based or tool-based services. A vCISO is a person — your person — who understands your business context, knows your systems, and makes security decisions on your behalf over time.

That said, a good vCISO relationship often starts with project-based work. You do a security assessment, realize you need ongoing guidance, and the relationship evolves into a retainer. That's a natural progression, not a sales pitch.
